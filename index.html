🏖️ Beaches & swimming

  • 🌅Porthcurnick BeachSheltered sandy cove a 10-min walk north. Home of The Hidden Hut. Great at low tide.
  • ⛵Tatams / the harbour beachRight in the village — sand at low tide, slipway, rock pools for the kids.
  • 🏄Towan BeachWide sandy beach near Porth Farm, ~1.5 mi south. National Trust car park.
  • 🐚Pendower & CarneLong golden sweep across the bay — join up at low tide for a mile of sand.

🍴 Eat & drink

  • 🔥The Hidden HutLegendary beach café at Porthcurnick. Day menu of pasties, wraps & cake; famous Feast Nights book out fast online.
  • 🍺The Plume of FeathersVillage pub in Portscatho — pints, pub food and a proper local welcome.
  • ☕The Boathouse / village shopsDeli, gallery and general store on the harbour for supplies and ice cream.
  • 🦞Fresh seafoodCrab and lobster still landed locally — ask on the harbour or at the shop.

🚗 Days out nearby

  • ⛵St MawesPretty harbour town with castle & ferry. ~15 min drive or a coast-path expedition.
  • 🌿St Just in RoselandOne of the most beautiful churchyards in England, tumbling to a tidal creek.
  • ⛴️King Harry Ferry → TrelissickChain ferry across the Fal to NT gardens and the road to Truro/Falmouth.
  • 🏰FalmouthMaritime museum, beaches and shops — around 45 min via the ferry.

Coastal walks

The South West Coast Path runs right through the village. Cliffs, wildflowers and sea views in both directions. Wear grippy shoes — paths can be muddy and near cliff edges.

Portscatho → Porthcurnick → Towan Beach

📏 ~2.5 mi return⏱️ 1–1.5 hrs🥾 Easy

Head north on the coast path past Porthcurnick Beach (pause at the Hidden Hut) and on to the sands at Towan. Big open sea views across Gerrans Bay. Return the same way or loop inland via Rosevine.

Portscatho → Gerrans → St Anthony Head

📏 ~7 mi⏱️ 3–4 hrs🥾 Moderate

A proper day's walk south to the tip of the peninsula. Lighthouse, WWII gun batteries, and views to St Mawes and Falmouth. Loop back inland through fields and lanes.

Gerrans Bay circular via Rosevine

📏 ~4 mi⏱️ 2 hrs🥾 Easy–Moderate

Coast path one way, quiet country lanes and footpaths back. Good for a shorter afternoon leg-stretch with a café stop.

Nare Head & Carne Beacon

📏 ~4–5 mi⏱️ 2–2.5 hrs🥾 Moderate

A short drive to the dramatic headland at Nare, then a wildflower cliff walk. Carne Beacon is one of the largest Bronze Age burial mounds in Britain.

Nature & wildlife

🐟 What's in the sea now

Seasons are guides — Cornish weather and local conditions shift them a little each year. 🎣 Right now is highlighted.

🐦 Birds on the cliff walks

  • 🖤ChoughCornwall's emblem — glossy black with a red curved bill & legs. Recovering along Roseland cliffs. Listen for the "chee-ow" call.
  • 🪶FulmarStiff-winged "tube-nose" gliding along the cliff faces spring–summer. Nests on ledges.
  • 🎯GannetHuge white seabird with black wingtips, diving offshore like an arrow — best on breezy summer days.
  • 🦅Peregrine & kestrelPeregrine (fastest bird alive) hunts the headlands; kestrels hover over the cliff-top grass.
  • 🧡StonechatLittle robin-shaped bird on gorse tops — call sounds like two pebbles knocking together.
  • ⚫Shag & cormorantBlack diving birds on the rocks, wings spread out to dry.
  • 🧡OystercatcherBlack-and-white wader, long orange bill, loud "peep-peep" along the shore.
  • 🐧Guillemots & razorbillsAuks rafting on the sea offshore, mainly spring/early summer.

🦭 Seals, dolphins & more

  • 🦭Grey sealsYear-round in Gerrans Bay — often a curious head bobbing near the harbour or off the rocks.
  • 🐬Dolphins & porpoiseCommon & bottlenose dolphins pass offshore; harbour porpoise year-round. Scan the horizon on calm days.
  • 🦈Basking sharkThe world's 2nd-largest fish — occasionally seen feeding at the surface May–July in warm calm spells.
  • 🪼JellyfishCompass, moon & barrel jellyfish drift in during summer. Harmless to admire; don't touch.
  • 🦀Rock poolsLow tide reveals shore crabs, blennies, anemones, prawns & starfish at Tatams and Porthcurnick.

🌸 Wildflowers & plants

The cliff paths blaze with colour spring to autumn: thrift (sea pink), kidney vetch, sea campion, gorse (coconut-scented), foxgloves, and in late summer purple heather. Look for rare lichens on the rocks — a sign of Cornwall's clean Atlantic air.

🌙 Stargazing: The Roseland has dark skies. On a clear night away from the harbour lights you can see the Milky Way — best in winter, or look for the summer Perseid meteors in August.

Fun facts & tips

💡 Did you know?

1
Two villages, one place. Portscatho (the harbour) and Gerrans (up the hill, around the church) effectively merge into one community — the parish is "Gerrans".
2
A pilchard past. Like much of Cornwall, Portscatho thrived on pilchard fishing. Old "fish cellars" once salted and pressed the catch for export.
3
Roseland ≠ roses. The name likely comes from the Cornish "rosinis" — meaning "promontory / heath isle" — not the flower.
4
On screen. The Roseland's timeless lanes and coves have featured in TV and film — its unspoilt look is the whole appeal.
5
Bronze Age giants. Carne Beacon above the bay is one of the largest Bronze Age barrows in Britain — legend says a king was buried there in a golden boat.
6
Gig racing country. Cornish pilot gigs (six-oared rowing boats) are a proud local tradition — you may spot crews training in the bay.

🎒 Practical tips

  • 🅿️ParkingVillage parking is limited — use the car park at the top and walk down. National Trust car parks at Porth Farm (Towan) & Carne.
  • 📶SignalMobile coverage is patchy on the cliffs — download maps offline before you set out.
  • 🐕DogsWelcome on the coast path; seasonal restrictions on some beaches in summer. Watch for cliff edges.
  • 🚿SwimmingHarbour & Porthcurnick are lovely, but there are no lifeguards — swim near others & respect the tide.
  • 🌡️Sea tempWarmest Aug–Sep (~16–17°C); bracing but swimmable May–Oct. A wetsuit extends the season.

🆘 Useful in an emergency

  • ☎️999 or 112For coastguard emergencies ask for the Coastguard. Note the nearest coast-path marker or beach name.
  • 🩹NHS 111Non-emergency medical help. Nearest minor injuries / hospital is in Truro (Royal Cornwall).
  • 🌊Rip currentsIf caught, don't fight it — float, raise an arm, and swim parallel to shore.
